3) WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES
==============================================================================

F1. THE P5 BUDGET IS LARGELY FICTION.
    Small draws 0.01 positives from s32 and medium 0.00, against pools of 0.62
    and 2.71 — and those few candidates carry an alignment metric of ~0, so
    they fail the metric>eps test regardless of budget. "Guarantee the coarse
    levels a share" therefore does nothing for 92% of the dataset. Only large
    can use s32 at all, and only for ~1.7 positives under stock.

F2. LB-TAL'S REAL MECHANISM IS THE P3 CAP.
    Since P5 cannot absorb budget and P4 saturates quickly, the only binding
    constraint any scheme applies is how many stride-8 positives it allows.
    uniform cuts small from 6.64 to 3.64; coarse244 to 1.96.

F3. THE EFFECT IS SINGLE-PEAKED IN THE P3 BUDGET.
    Cross-referencing the training results (test split, mAP50-95):

      P3 budget   config                            mAP50-95
          8       lb_prop -> {8:8,16:2,32:1}          0.5482
         ~6.6     stock (global top-10)               0.5477
          5       lb_uniform_tk13 -> ceil(13/3)       0.5542
          4       lb_uniform                          0.5557   <- peak
          3       NOT TESTED                             ?
          2       lb_coarse_244                       0.5534

    The gain is NOT more supply and NOT less supply — small objects perform
    best at ~5.7 total positives with a specific fine/coarse mix. Giving them
    MORE P3 than stock (lb_prop, P3=8) is worth nothing (0.5482 vs 0.5477).
    Only P3=3 is untested.

F4. SMALL OBJECTS ARE SUPPLY-LIMITED AT P4; MEDIUM AND LARGE ARE BUDGET-LIMITED.
                  s16 pool   sel stock   sel LB-TAL   limited by
      small           2.46        1.08         2.08   SUPPLY
      medium         10.63        3.39         3.87   budget
      large         125.28        7.96         3.92   budget
    Consequence: raising the P4 budget cannot meaningfully move small objects
    (p4wide nudged them 2.08 -> 2.27, i.e. 85% -> 92% of an already-small
    pool), but it directly controls medium and large.

F5. LARGE OBJECTS LIVE ON P4, NOT P5.
    7.96 of large's 9.79 stock positives come from s16. Under uniform that is
    HALVED to 3.92 — the single biggest change in the whole cross-tab — purely
    because the budget is 4.

F6. THE PER-LEVEL BUDGET FORCES JUNK POSITIVES ONTO LARGE OBJECTS.
    Stock selects 0.16 stride-8 anchors for a large GT. uniform selects 2.91,
    because P3 must hand out 4 slots and a large object has 501 candidates
    there to fill them with. A stride-8 anchor on a >96px object is a poor
    match, and large's quality collapses accordingly: mean IoU 0.876 -> 0.720,
    p10 IoU 0.707 -> 0.387, %IoU<0.3 from 0.1% to 5.6%.

F7. THAT QUALITY COLLAPSE IS SURVIVABLE ALONE, FATAL IN COMBINATION.
    Under lb_uniform the degraded medium/large positives did NOT cost mAP:
    medium 0.6532 vs baseline 0.6507, large 0.5775 vs 0.5773 — both flat. The
    network absorbs them. But cmb_lbU_swa0703 (sqrt0703 + uniform) scored
    0.5510, worse than either parent (0.5564 / 0.5557), with large falling to
    0.5437. sqrt weighting ALSO lowers large's loss weight, so large gets junk
    positives AND less gradient per positive, and cannot recover. Two
    independent suppressions of the same bucket.
    NOTE: a degraded diagnostic metric is not automatically a degraded result.
    F6 only matters because of the interaction in F7.

F8. p4wide {8:4,16:7,32:1} REPAIRS MEDIUM, HALF-REPAIRS LARGE.
      medium  %IoU<0.3  4.6% -> 1.0%   (stock 0.0%),  P4 3.87 -> 5.80,
              total positives 8.54 -> 9.54 against stock's 9.87
      large   P4 3.92 -> 6.79, mean IoU 0.720 -> 0.761
      small   unchanged to marginally better (P4 2.08 -> 2.27)
    But large's %IoU<0.3 went 5.6% -> 6.1% and p10 IoU only 0.387 -> 0.400.
    The residue is large's 2.17 remaining stride-8 positives.

F9. ONE GLOBAL BUDGET CANNOT SERVE ALL THREE SIZES.
    Fixing large needs a LOW P3 budget; small peaks at P3=4. The budget is
    global, so the two demands are irreconcilable within this mechanism. Two
    ways out:
      (a) the quality gate, which filters by METRIC rather than by budget: a
          large GT's best metric is on P4, so its P3 picks fall below
          gate*global_best and are pruned, while a small GT's best IS on P3 so
          its picks survive. Effectively size-conditional, for free.
      (b) a genuinely size-conditional per-level budget in the assigner. Not
          implemented; it would dominate any single global 3-tuple.

F10. balanced_capped IS lb_coarse_244.
    At topk=10 with 3 levels it resolves to {8:2,16:4,32:4} — bit for bit the
    coarse244 budget, already run at 0.5534. Its docstring claims it "protects
    the coarse levels", but P5 gets the same 4 uniform already gave it; the
    only thing that changes is P3 dropping 4 -> 2. lb_balcap and lb_balcap_qg50
    were removed from the sweep as duplicates rather than re-measured.

F11. s32 IS METRIC-BIASED UNDER STOCK, NOT MERELY STARVED.
    Section 1 SEL BIAS (sel share / cand share):
                    s8     s16     s32
      stock       0.94    1.42    0.34   <- s32 flagged METRIC-BIASED
      uniform     0.70    2.11    1.30
      coarse244   0.48    2.74    2.29
      p4wide      0.64    2.65    0.16
    Values near 1.0 mean the starvation is purely geometric and a per-level
    budget is the right fix; below 1.0 means the alignment METRIC additionally
    penalises the level, which a budget cannot repair. The per-level schemes do
    lift s32 above 1.0, i.e. the geometric part is addressed. The residual
    metric bias is what an LBA prior would target — use_lba is NOT implemented
    in lossv2updated.py (it lives in loss_custom_v3_fixed.py).

F12. PER-CLASS BEHAVIOUR IS UNIFORM ACROSS THE THREE CLASSES.
    Stock SEL BIAS by class:  backpack 0.92/1.44/0.45, bag 0.96/1.19/0.85,
    trolley 0.94/1.49/0.09. The classes differ at s32 (trolley 0.09 is the
    extreme) but track each other closely at s8/s16 under every scheme. The
    bag class's weakness (AP50-95 0.4666 vs trolley 0.6185) is therefore NOT
    an assignment-geometry problem — it has to be attacked on the cls side.


==============================================================================
4) THE FINDING THAT DID NOT COME FROM THIS FILE
==============================================================================
Across all 18 training runs, AR50_small ~0.95 while R50_small ~0.70. The
detections for nearly all small objects EXIST; they score below the F1-optimal
threshold. That is a confidence/ranking failure, and nothing in the assignment
axis addresses it — 25 pp of headroom against the ~0.9 pp the whole LB-TAL
line has been contesting. Per class the gap is worst for bag (AR50_s 0.9352 ->
R50_s 0.62, a 31 pp gap), consistent with F12.

Also untouched: the mAP50 -> mAP50-95 ratio is 0.65 for small vs 0.75 for
medium, and it moved only 0.649 -> 0.657 across all 18 runs. Small-box
localisation has never been attacked; use_nwd was False in every run.

==============================================================================
6) CORRECTIONS TO EARLIER READINGS OF THIS DATA — recorded deliberately
==============================================================================
  * "The per-level cap starves small objects and that is bad." Wrong on the
    consequence. The cap is real (F2) but the outcome curve (F3) shows the cut
    HELPS up to P3=4. lb_prop gives small more P3 than stock and gains nothing.
  * "The LB-TAL axis reduces to the P3 budget." Overstated. It was inferred
    from uniform vs coarse244 having identical s16 — but both have P4=4, so
    P3 was varied while P4 was held fixed. F4/F5 show P4 matters a great deal.
  * "balanced_capped protects large." It does not; see F10. P5 keeps the same
    4 slots uniform gave it.
  * "Signal B: over-boosting small collapsed large." Small went DOWN in cmb
    (0.5047 vs uniform's 0.5085). There was no small-boost to trade against
    large. The real mechanism is F6+F7.
  * "raising P4 cannot touch small at all." Nearly right — small moved 2.08 ->
    2.27 because it was at 85% of its P4 pool, not 100%.

SEED NOISE, for reading any of the training numbers above: 0.12 pp on overall
mAP50-95 and 2.06 pp on large mAP50-95, measured from lb_uniform vs
lb_uniform_seed1 (same config, seeds 0 and 1). Differences below those are not
rankings. The large-object claims in F6/F7 rest on FOUR correlated metrics
moving together (P4 supply, mean IoU, p10 IoU, %<0.3), not on any single
number clearing that bar.
